# Day 37 : Passive Voice in Business & Academic Writing

## 📚 Overview
In academic research, executive briefings, and formal correspondence, tone and objectivity are paramount. The passive voice plays a central role in scholarly and professional communication: it depersonalizes statements, establishes an objective stance, shields communicators from making personal accusations, and foregrounds findings over individuals. Understanding when and how to deploy passive structures—especially impersonal passive constructions—elevates the sophistication and authority of your writing.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Formulate impersonal passive constructions (*"It is estimated that...", "The findings are believed to indicate..."*).
- Frame objective methodology and results sections in academic and technical papers.
- Write tactful, blameless business communications that emphasize resolutions rather than individual faults.
- Discern when passive voice enhances formal authority and when active voice is necessary to prevent bureaucratic ambiguity.

## 📖 Theoretical Breakdown

### 1. The Rhetorical Functions of Passive in Formal Registers

| Register | Objective | Example Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Academic Research** | Emphasize reproducibility and experimental procedure over the identity of researchers. | *Ten blood samples were centrifuged at 3,000 RPM for 15 minutes.* |
| **Scientific Discussion** | Distinguish between empirical facts and tentative interpretations. | *The discrepancy is considered to be a consequence of thermal fluctuation.* |
| **Business Reporting** | Highlight financial performance, strategic outcomes, and quarterly targets. | *Net operating profits were increased by 14.8% during the fourth quarter.* |
| **Corporate Diplomacy** | Address errors, delays, or contract violations diplomatically without personal hostility. | *A payment discrepancy was identified during the routine vendor reconciliation.* |

---

### 2. Impersonal Passive Reporting Structures
When reporting widely held opinions, estimates, or findings without attributing them to a specific named individual, English employs two key impersonal passive structures:

#### Structure A: `It + passive reporting verb + that-clause`
* Reporting verbs commonly used: *believed, alleged, reported, estimated, expected, acknowledged, assumed, observed.*
* **Active:** Economists estimate that the inflation rate will decline by 2%.
* **Impersonal Passive:** **It is estimated that** the inflation rate will decline by 2%.
* *Other examples:*
  - *It has been observed that* user engagement drops after five seconds of latency.
  - *It was widely reported that* the merger negotiations were underway.

#### Structure B: `Subject + passive reporting verb + to-infinitive`
* **Active:** Experts consider the new renewable turbine to be the most efficient design.
* **Passive:** The new renewable turbine **is considered to be** the most efficient design.
* **Active:** Observers expect the market to stabilize next quarter.
* **Passive:** The market **is expected to stabilize** next quarter.
* With completed past events:
  - *The prototype **is reported to have completed** all endurance evaluations.*

---

### 3. Diplomatic Rephrasing in Professional Communication
Compare confrontational active phrasing with constructive, professional passive phrasing:

| Direct / Confrontational Active | Tactful & Constructive Passive |
| :--- | :--- |
| *"You forgot to include the client attachments."* | *"The client attachments **were omitted** from the previous email."* |
| *"Your team missed the deadline on Friday."* | *"The deadline **was not met** on Friday; let us recalibrate our milestones."* |
| *"You miscalculated our tax deduction."* | *"A recalculation **is required** to rectify the deduction figure."* |

---

### 4. Avoiding "Bureaucratic Fog" (When NOT to Overuse Passive)
While passive creates distance and objectivity, over-reliance can obscure accountability:
* **Ambiguous / Evidentiary Deficit:** *"Mistakes were made."* (Who made them? Who is responsible for fixing them?)
* **Overly Clumsy:** *"A decision was reached by the members of the management committee that the implementation of the policy should be effected."*
* **Streamlined Executive Clarity:** *"The management committee decided to implement the policy immediately."*
> **Principle:** Use the passive voice to highlight the result, preserve objectivity, or foster diplomatic goodwill; use the active voice when clarity of agency and accountability is mandatory.

## 💬 Conversational Dialogue

**Context:** Dr. Aris Thorne (Research Director) and Megan Vance (Senior Policy Analyst) are reviewing a draft submission for an international public health symposium.

* **Dr. Thorne:** "Megan, I read through Section 3 of your manuscript. You wrote: *'We surveyed 4,000 households and we analyzed the drinking water.'*"
* **Megan:** "Yes, Dr. Thorne. Should that be phrased with more scientific detachment?"
* **Dr. Thorne:** "Precisely. In clinical journals, the methodology must remain impersonal: *'A total of 4,000 households were surveyed, and community water supplies were systematically analyzed.'*"
* **Megan:** "That makes sense. It highlights the sample size and experimental rigour rather than our research team."
* **Dr. Thorne:** "Exactly. Furthermore, in the conclusions, instead of saying *'People believe the intervention worked'*, write: *'The intervention is believed to have reduced transmission rates substantially.'*"
* **Megan:** "Understood. The impersonal passive provides academic gravity and precise rhetorical calibration."

## 💡 Practical Tip for Daily Practice
> In academic papers and formal proposals, audit your draft with the **"I / We" scan**: search for first-person pronouns (*I, we, our*). Wherever you find them in methodology or data reporting, convert the sentence into an objective passive structure to maintain scholarly detachment.
