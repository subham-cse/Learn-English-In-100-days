# Day 36 : Active Voice vs. Passive Voice

## 📚 Overview
In English, voice indicates whether the subject of a sentence performs the action (**Active Voice**) or receives the action (**Passive Voice**). While active voice creates clear, direct, and energetic sentences, passive voice shifts attention to the recipient of the action or the action itself. Mastering how to construct and transition between active and passive constructions across tenses gives you stylistic flexibility and communicative precision.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Identify active and passive voice constructions instantly across various contexts.
- Convert sentences between active and passive voice across all major English tenses and modals.
- Understand strategic reasons for selecting passive voice (focus on result, unknown/unimportant agent, tactfulness).
- Avoid common passive voice pitfalls such as dangling modifiers and unnatural sentence structures.

---

## 📖 Theoretical Breakdown

### 1. Core Concept & Fundamental Architecture
* **Active Voice:** The grammatical subject **performs** the action.
  $$\text{Subject (Agent)} + \text{Verb} + \text{Object (Receiver)}$$
  *Example:* *The architect designed the innovative bridge.*

* **Passive Voice:** The grammatical subject **receives** the action. The original agent becomes an optional prepositional phrase introduced by **"by"**.
  $$\text{Object as New Subject} + [\text{Be in tense of original verb}] + \text{Past Participle (V3)} + [\text{by Agent}]$$
  *Example:* *The innovative bridge was designed by the architect.*

---

### 2. Full Tense Transformation Chart

| Tense | Active Voice Construction | Passive Voice Construction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Simple Present** | She **writes** the quarterly report. | The quarterly report **is written** (by her). |
| **Present Continuous** | The crew **is painting** the bridge. | The bridge **is being painted** (by the crew). |
| **Simple Past** | Alexander Fleming **discovered** penicillin in 1928. | Penicillin **was discovered** by Alexander Fleming in 1928. |
| **Past Continuous** | Technicians **were repairing** the servers at midnight. | The servers **were being repaired** at midnight. |
| **Present Perfect** | The committee **has approved** the budget. | The budget **has been approved** (by the committee). |
| **Past Perfect** | They **had finalized** the contract before lunch. | The contract **had been finalized** before lunch. |
| **Simple Future (`will`)** | The courier **will deliver** the parcel tomorrow. | The parcel **will be delivered** tomorrow. |
| **Future (`be going to`)** | The firm **is going to launch** a new app. | A new app **is going to be launched**. |
| **Modal Verbs (`can / must / should`)** | You **must submit** the application today. | The application **must be submitted** today. |
| **Modal Perfects (`should have`)** | They **should have sent** the notice earlier. | The notice **should have been sent** earlier. |

> **Note on Intransitive Verbs:** Verbs that do not take a direct object (e.g., *arrive, sleep, occur, die, disappear, happen*) **cannot** be transformed into passive voice.
> - *Incorrect:* An accident was happened yesterday.
> - *Correct:* An accident happened yesterday.

---

### 3. When and Why to Use Passive Voice
Passive voice is not grammatically inferior; rather, it is a deliberate rhetorical choice used when:
1. **The agent is unknown or irrelevant:**
   * *My bicycle was stolen last night.* (We do not know who stole it.)
2. **The receiver or result is the primary focus:**
   * *Over ten thousand trees were planted across the reserve.* (The trees and conservation effort matter more than the volunteers' names.)
3. **Professional tact, diplomacy, and avoiding blame:**
   * *A clerical error was made in the invoice.* (Politer and less accusatory than *"You made an error."*)
4. **Scientific and factual reporting:**
   * *The solution was heated to 100°C.* (The experimenter's identity does not alter the physical truth.)

## 💬 Conversational Dialogue

**Context:** Clara (Product Lead) and Julian (Senior Developer) are reviewing project deliverables before a stakeholder showcase.

* **Clara:** "Good morning, Julian. Has the security audit been completed yet?"
* **Julian:** "Yes, Clara. The security scan **was conducted** yesterday afternoon, and all critical vulnerabilities **have been patched**."
* **Clara:** "Excellent. What about the client dashboard? Were the requested charts updated?"
* **Julian:** "The charts **are being integrated** right now by Liam. They **will be deployed** to the staging environment before 3:00 PM."
* **Clara:** "Perfect. Notice how much clearer our status reports sound when we emphasize what has been completed rather than who handled every tiny ticket."
* **Julian:** "Absolutely. When we present to the executive board, the accomplishments **are highlighted** instantly."

## ✍️ Practice Exercises

### Exercise A: Transform Active into Passive
Convert the following sentences into natural passive voice. Keep the original tense intact. Include the agent (`by ...`) only if it provides critical context.
1. The engineering department inspects every engine before shipment.
2. The graphic artist is designing the new promotional brochure.
3. The city council approved the construction permit yesterday.
4. Someone has spilled hot coffee all over the conference table.
5. The catering team will deliver the refreshments at noon.

### Exercise B: Identify and Correct the Error
Each sentence contains an error involving voice or past participle forms. Identify and fix it.
6. The historical monument was build in the fourteenth century.
7. Sudden storms were occurred along the coastline throughout the winter.
8. The package has being delivered to the wrong address.
9. All confidential documents must be shred immediately after review.

---

## 🔑 Self-Check Answer Key

### Exercise A Answers:
1. **Every engine is inspected (by the engineering department) before shipment.** *(Simple Present: is + inspected)*
2. **The new promotional brochure is being designed by the graphic artist.** *(Present Continuous: is being + designed)*
3. **The construction permit was approved by the city council yesterday.** *(Simple Past: was + approved)*
4. **Hot coffee has been spilled all over the conference table.** *(Present Perfect: has been + spilled; agent "someone" is omitted as it is vague).*
5. **The refreshments will be delivered (by the catering team) at noon.** *(Simple Future: will be + delivered)*

### Exercise B Answers:
6. **Error:** *build* $\rightarrow$ **Correction:** The historical monument was **built** in the fourteenth century. *(Irregular past participle of build is built).*
7. **Error:** *were occurred* $\rightarrow$ **Correction:** Sudden storms **occurred** along the coastline throughout the winter. *(Occur is an intransitive verb; passive voice is ungrammatical).*
8. **Error:** *has being* $\rightarrow$ **Correction:** The package **has been** delivered to the wrong address. *(Present Perfect Passive uses "has been", not "has being").*
9. **Error:** *shred* (present form) $\rightarrow$ **Correction:** All confidential documents must be **shredded** immediately after review. *(Modal passive requires modal + be + V3).*

## 💡 Practical Tip for Daily Practice
> **The "By Zombies" Test:** If you are unsure whether a sentence is in the passive voice, add the phrase **"by zombies"** right after the verb. If the sentence remains grammatically coherent, it is in the passive voice!
> - *The proposal was approved (by zombies).* $\rightarrow$ Passive!
> - *The team approved (by zombies) the proposal.* $\rightarrow$ Active (does not fit grammatically after verb without changing sentence structure).
