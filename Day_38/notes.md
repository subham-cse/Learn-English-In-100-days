# Day 38 : Direct Speech vs. Reported Speech

## 📚 Overview
When communicating what another person expressed, we can quote their exact words using **Direct Speech** (*She said, "I am ready."*) or report their message indirectly using **Reported Speech** (*She said that she was ready.*). Moving from direct to reported speech requires a systematic sequence of adjustments: **backshifting verb tenses**, shifting **pronouns**, and recalculating **time and place references**. Mastering reported speech is vital for storytelling, news reporting, meeting minutes, and professional discussions.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Distinguish between direct quotes and reported speech structures.
- Apply backshifting rules accurately across all primary tenses and modal auxiliaries.
- Identify scenarios where backshifting is optional or omitted (universal truths, ongoing relevance).
- Modify pronouns, possessive adjectives, and adverbial time/place markers seamlessly.
- Use reporting verbs accurately (specifically distinguishing *say* vs. *tell*).

## 📖 Theoretical Breakdown

### 1. Direct vs. Indirect Architecture
* **Direct Speech:** Preserves the speaker's exact words inside quotation marks.
  - *Elena said, "I have finalized the presentation."*
* **Reported Speech:** Paraphrases the speaker's words without quotation marks. The subordinate clause is introduced by an optional conjunction **"that"**.
  - *Elena said (that) she had finalized the presentation.*

---

### 2. The Mechanics of Tense Backshifting
When the reporting verb is in the past tense (*said, told, mentioned, explained*), the verb in the reported clause typically shifts one step back into the past.

| Direct Speech Tense | Direct Speech Example | Reported Speech Tense | Reported Speech Transformation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Simple Present** | *"I work in marketing."* | **Simple Past** | He said (that) he **worked** in marketing. |
| **Present Continuous** | *"We are developing an app."* | **Past Continuous** | They said they **were developing** an app. |
| **Simple Past** | *"I visited the site."* | **Past Perfect** | She said she **had visited** the site. |
| **Past Continuous** | *"I was reviewing files."* | **Past Perfect Continuous** | He said he **had been reviewing** files. |
| **Present Perfect** | *"We have reached our target."*| **Past Perfect** | They said they **had reached** their target. |
| **Past Perfect** | *"I had left before noon."* | **Past Perfect** *(No shift)*| She said she **had left** before noon. |
| **Future (`will`)** | *"I will email the contract."* | **Conditional (`would`)** | He said he **would email** the contract. |
| **Modal: `can`** | *"I can fix the issue."* | **Modal: `could`** | She said she **could fix** the issue. |
| **Modal: `may`** | *"It may rain tomorrow."* | **Modal: `might`** | He said it **might rain** the next day. |
| **Modal: `must / have to`**| *"I must finish tonight."* | **`had to`** | She said she **had to finish** that night. |

> **Modals that do NOT shift:** *would, could, should, might, ought to, used to* remain unchanged in reported speech.

---

### 3. When NOT to Backshift
Backshifting is **not required** when:
1. **The statement represents a universal scientific truth or permanent general fact:**
   - *Direct:* "Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius."
   - *Reported:* The instructor explained that water **boils** at 100 degrees Celsius.
2. **The situation remains current and true at the moment of reporting:**
   - *Direct:* "I live in Berlin." (Said an hour ago, still lives there)
   - *Reported:* Mark said that he **lives** in Berlin. *(Both "lives" and "lived" are acceptable).*
3. **The reporting verb is in the present tense:**
   - *Direct:* "I am delighted to join the team."
   - *Reported:* She **says** that she **is** delighted to join the team.

---

### 4. Shifting Pronouns and Adverbial Expressions of Time & Place

Because the reporting perspective shifts from the original speaker to the narrator, pronouns and deictic markers (time and place words) must adapt:

#### Pronoun Adaptations
- `I / me / my` $\rightarrow$ `he / she / him / her / his / hers`
- `we / us / our` $\rightarrow$ `they / them / their`
- `you` $\rightarrow$ `I / he / she / they` (depending on the addressed listener)

#### Time & Place Reference Shifts

| Direct Speech Form | Reported Speech Equivalent |
| :--- | :--- |
| **now** | *then / at that time* |
| **today / tonight** | *that day / that night* |
| **yesterday** | *the day before / the previous day* |
| **tomorrow** | *the following day / the next day* |
| **last week / month** | *the previous week / month* |
| **next week / month** | *the following week / month* |
| **ago** | *before* |
| **this / these** | *that / those* |
| **here** | *there* |

---

### 5. Reporting Verbs: `Say` vs. `Tell`
- **Say:** Does not require a personal indirect object.
  - *Correct:* She **said (that)** the server was down.
  - *Incorrect:* She said me that the server was down.
  - *With object:* She said **to me** that... (rare in modern business writing).
- **Tell:** Requires a personal direct/indirect object indicating who received the message.
  - *Correct:* She **told me (that)** the server was down.
  - *Incorrect:* She told that the server was down.

## 💬 Conversational Dialogue

**Context:** Maya is debriefing her teammate Carlos on what their enterprise client said in an earlier conference call.

* **Maya:** "Carlos, I just finished the debriefing call with Mr. Henderson."
* **Carlos:** "Great. What did he say about our proposal?"
* **Maya:** "He said that he **had reviewed** the budget with his executive board the day before."
* **Carlos:** "Did he approve the timeline?"
* **Maya:** "He told me that they **were** very pleased with the deliverables, but he mentioned that they **would need** two additional days to approve the final scope."
* **Carlos:** "Did he indicate when we would receive the signed agreement?"
* **Maya:** "Yes, he promised that his team **would send** the documentation by tomorrow afternoon—meaning later today for us."
* **Carlos:** "Fantastic news! Thanks for summarizing his exact points so clearly."

## ✍️ Practice Exercises

### Exercise A: Convert Direct to Reported Speech
Convert each statement into reported speech using the past reporting verb provided. Apply all necessary tense, pronoun, and time/place transformations.
1. "I will call you tomorrow morning," Lucas said to Sarah.  
   *(Lucas told Sarah that...)*
2. "We have lived in Toronto for ten years," the couple stated.  
   *(The couple stated that...)*
3. "I am preparing the annual financial review right now," Rachel remarked.  
   *(Rachel remarked that...)*
4. "The team completed the testing two days ago," the manager reported.  
   *(The manager reported that...)*
5. "You must wear a protective badge inside this laboratory," the guard informed us.  
   *(The guard informed us that...)*

### Exercise B: Choose the Correct Reporting Verb (`said` vs. `told`)
6. Marcus ______ that the flight had been rescheduled.
7. Marcus ______ us that the flight had been rescheduled.
8. The consultant ______ to the committee that costs had surged.

## 🔑 Self-Check Answer Key

### Exercise A:
1. **Lucas told Sarah that he would call her the following morning (or the next morning).**
2. **The couple stated that they had lived in Toronto for ten years.**
3. **Rachel remarked that she was preparing the annual financial review then (or at that time).**
4. **The manager reported that the team had completed the testing two days before (or two days previously).**
5. **The guard informed us that we had to wear a protective badge inside that laboratory.**

### Exercise B:
6. Marcus **said** that the flight had been rescheduled. *(No personal object follows).*
7. Marcus **told** us that the flight had been rescheduled. *(Personal object "us" follows).*
8. The consultant **said** to the committee that costs had surged. *(Uses "said to [object]").*

## 💡 Practical Tip for Daily Practice
> **The Perspective Pivot Test:** Whenever you report speech, imagine you are a movie narrator standing outside the room hours later. Ask yourself three questions:
> 1. *Who is speaking now?* (Adjust pronouns: *I* becomes *he/she*).
> 2. *When did the event occur relative to now?* (Step the verb one notch backward).
> 3. *Does 'here' and 'today' still apply?* (If not, switch to *there* and *that day*).
