# Day 45 : Phrasal Verbs with Three Words

## 📚 Overview
Phrasal-prepositional verbs—commonly known as **three-word phrasal verbs**—consist of a base verb followed by an adverbial particle and a preposition:
$$\text{Verb} + \text{Particle} + \text{Preposition} \quad (\text{e.g., } \textit{come} + \textit{up} + \textit{with})$$
These multi-word idioms are indispensable in everyday English, business strategy, and casual discussions. From a grammatical perspective, they are remarkably consistent: **they are strictly transitive and 100% inseparable**. Today, we master high-impact three-word expressions and conquer the famous grammatical pitfall of *"look forward to + -ing"*.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Formulate three-word phrasal verbs with correct word order without splitting particles.
- Apply the **Gerund Rule** correctly after `look forward to` (*look forward to hearing from you*, never *to hear*).
- Express tolerance (*put up with*), innovation (*come up with*), and depletion (*run out of*) fluently.
- Identify and avoid common learner traps such as misplaced pronouns or omitted prepositions.

---

## 📖 Theoretical Breakdown

### 1. The Cardinal Rule: Absolute Inseparability
Unlike two-word separable phrasal verbs (*turn it off*), three-word phrasal verbs **cannot be separated under any circumstance**. The object (whether a noun, a name, or a pronoun) must **always** appear directly after the final preposition:

$$\text{Verb} + \text{Particle} + \text{Preposition} + \mathbf{Direct\ Object\ (Noun / Pronoun)}$$

| Correct Structure | Ungrammatical Separation |
| :--- | :--- |
| *She **came up with** a solution.* $\checkmark$ | *She came a solution up with.* ❌ |
| *I cannot **put up with** it anymore.* $\checkmark$ | *I cannot put it up with anymore.* ❌ |
| *We **ran out of** fuel.* $\checkmark$ | *We ran fuel out of.* ❌ |
| *They **got away with** it.* $\checkmark$ | *They got it away with.* ❌ |

---

### 2. High-Frequency Three-Word Phrasal Verbs

| Phrasal Verb | Meaning | Latinate Synonym | Practical Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **come up with** | Devise, invent, or produce an idea/solution | *Create / Devise* | *Our engineering team **came up with** a novel cooling design.* |
| **look forward to** | Anticipate future events with eager excitement | *Anticipate* | *We **look forward to working** with your team.* |
| **put up with** | Tolerate or endure unpleasant circumstances | *Tolerate / Endure* | *I refuse to **put up with** such rude behavior.* |
| **run out of** | Exhaust or deplete the available supply of something | *Deplete / Exhaust* | *We have **run out of** printer paper.* |
| **get away with** | Escape blame, penalty, or consequence for wrongdoing | *Evade penalty* | *He thought he could **get away with** plagiarism.* |
| **cut down on** | Reduce intake, volume, or consumption | *Reduce / Curtail* | *The doctor advised him to **cut down on** refined sugar.* |
| **keep up with** | Maintain pace with rapid changes or competitors | *Match pace* | *It is difficult to **keep up with** daily AI breakthroughs.* |
| **stand up for** | Champion or defend a principle or person | *Defend / Advocate* | *Always **stand up for** your principles.* |

---

### 3. The "Look Forward To" Gerund Rule ⚠️
In the expression `look forward to`, the word **"to"** is a **preposition**, not an infinitive marker. Prepositions in English must be followed by a **noun phrase** or a **gerund (`verb + -ing`)**:

$$\text{look forward to} + \mathbf{Noun / Gerund\ (-ing)}$$

* ❌ **Incorrect:** *I look forward to hear from you.*
* ✅ **Correct:** *I look forward to **hearing** from you.*
* ❌ **Incorrect:** *We look forward to meet the new director.*
* ✅ **Correct:** *We look forward to **meeting** the new director.*
* ✅ **Correct with Noun:** *We look forward to **the conference**.*

## 💬 Conversational Dialogue

**Context:** Aaron (Operations Director) and Priya (Product Manager) are addressing an emergency supply shortage before an upcoming product release.

* **Aaron:** "Priya, do you have a minute? We have a serious bottleneck: our primary fulfillment center just **ran out of** sustainable packaging boxes."
* **Priya:** "Already? We ordered two thousand units last week. We cannot afford to delay shipments."
* **Aaron:** "Exactly. I can't **put up with** these continuous vendor delays any longer. We need to **come up with** an alternative supplier by midday."
* **Priya:** "I've already contacted a regional green-packaging partner. They agreed to deliver emergency inventory tomorrow morning."
* **Aaron:** "Brilliant! You always manage to **come up with** creative resolutions under pressure."
* **Priya:** "Thanks Aaron. Our customers have high standards, and we must **stand up for** our eco-friendly commitments."
* **Aaron:** "Agreed. I **look forward to reviewing** their contract once the shipment arrives."

## ✍️ Practice Exercises

### Exercise A: Complete with the Appropriate Three-Word Phrasal Verb
Fill in the blanks with the correct form of *run out of, come up with, look forward to, put up with, cut down on*.
1. Because of rising inflation, our family had to ______ unnecessary luxury expenditures.
2. The creative team spent three hours brainstorming and finally ______ a brilliant campaign slogan.
3. The motorist pulled into the nearest petrol station right before the car ______ fuel.
4. How can you ______ the loud construction noise outside your window all day long?
5. All the employees are ______ taking a well-deserved vacation this summer.

### Exercise B: Identify and Correct the Errors
6. "I look forward to attend the annual symposium next month."
7. "She couldn't put that irritating noise up with."
8. "He managed to get away the crime without being detected."

---

## 🔑 Self-Check Answer Key

### Exercise A:
1. **cut down on** *(Reduce expenditures).*
2. **came up with** *(Devised/created an idea).*
3. **ran out of** *(Exhausted supply).*
4. **put up with** *(Tolerate/endure).*
5. **looking forward to** *(Anticipating eagerly; followed by gerund "taking").*

### Exercise B:
6. **Error:** Infinitive verb after preposition $\rightarrow$ **Correction:** I look forward to **attending** the annual symposium next month.
7. **Error:** Separated three-word verb $\rightarrow$ **Correction:** She couldn't **put up with** that irritating noise. *(Never split three-word phrasal verbs).*
8. **Error:** Missing preposition $\rightarrow$ **Correction:** He managed to **get away with** the crime without being detected.
