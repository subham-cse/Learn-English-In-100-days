# Day 39 : Reported Questions, Orders, and Requests

## 📚 Overview
Reporting questions, commands, and polite requests differs significantly from reporting simple declarative statements. In reported questions, question word order and auxiliary inversion disappear, reverting to standard statement order (**Subject + Verb**). For orders, commands, and requests, sentences transform into concise **infinitive structures** (`to + verb` or `not to + verb`). Mastering these patterns prevents the common trap of keeping interrogative word order in reported clauses.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Convert Yes/No direct questions into indirect questions using **if** or **whether**.
- Report Wh- questions using correct affirmative word order (**Question Word + Subject + Verb**) without auxiliary inversion.
- Transform direct orders and instructions into reported speech using `told / ordered / commanded + object + to-infinitive`.
- Transform polite requests into reported speech using `asked / requested + object + to-infinitive`.
- Eliminate the direct question mark and auxiliary words (`do, does, did`) from indirect structures.

---

## 📖 Theoretical Breakdown

### 1. Reported Questions: The Statement Word Order Rule
In direct speech, questions invert the auxiliary verb and subject:
$$\text{Auxiliary} + \text{Subject} + \text{Main Verb}? \quad \longrightarrow \quad \text{"Where do you live?"}$$

In reported speech, the clause becomes an embedded noun clause, reverting to **statement word order** with **no auxiliary *do/does/did*** and **no question mark**:
$$\text{Subject} + \text{Verb} \quad \longrightarrow \quad \text{He asked where I lived.}$$

> ⚠️ **Common Mistake:**
> - *Incorrect:* He asked me where did I live?
> - *Incorrect:* She asked what was my name.
> - *Correct:* She asked **what my name was**.

---

### 2. Yes/No Questions: Using `If` and `Whether`
When reporting questions that expect a "yes" or "no" response, link the reported clause with **if** or **whether**.

| Direct Question | Reported Question (Backshifted) | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *"Do you speak French?"* | He asked **if** I spoke French. | Auxiliary *do* drops; verb backshifts to simple past. |
| *"Did you receive my email?"* | She asked **whether** I had received her email. | *Whether* is often preferred in formal business. |
| *"Are you attending the seminar?"* | Marcus inquired **if** we were attending the seminar. | Word order: Subject (*we*) + Verb (*were attending*). |
| *"Can you deliver by Monday?"* | The client asked **whether** they could deliver by Monday. | *Can* $\rightarrow$ *could*. |

---

### 3. Wh- Questions: Question Word + Subject + Verb
For information questions beginning with *who, what, where, when, why, which, how*, retain the question word as the connective conjunction:

| Direct Question | Reported Question |
| :--- | :--- |
| *"Where are the keys?"* | She asked where the keys **were**. |
| *"Why did you resign?"* | They asked why he **had resigned**. |
| *"How long will the upgrade take?"* | The manager asked how long the upgrade **would take**. |
| *"When does the next train depart?"* | The traveler asked when the next train **departed**. |

---

### 4. Reported Orders & Commands: `Verb + Object + (not) to-Infinitive`
Direct imperatives (*"Sit down!", "Don't touch that!"*) convert into an infinitive phrase.

* **Reporting verbs:** *order, tell, instruct, command, forbid, warn*.
* **Formula:**
  $$\text{Reporting Verb} + \text{Object (Person)} + \text{(not) to} + \text{Base Verb}$$

| Direct Imperative | Reported Order |
| :--- | :--- |
| *"Submit your passport immediately."* | The officer **ordered him to submit** his passport immediately. |
| *"Do not open this attachment."* | IT security **instructed us not to open** the attachment. |
| *"Turn off your cell phones."* | The proctor **told the candidates to turn off** their phones. |

---

### 5. Reported Requests: Polite Prompts
Direct polite requests (*"Could you please...", "Would you mind...", "Please..."*) also collapse into the `asked + object + to-infinitive` structure. The polite filler words (*please, could you, would you*) are omitted.

| Direct Polite Request | Reported Request |
| :--- | :--- |
| *"Could you please email me the spreadsheet?"* | She **asked me to email** her the spreadsheet. |
| *"Please do not park in this driveway."* | The homeowner **asked us not to park** in the driveway. |
| *"Would you mind closing the window?"* | He **asked her to close** the window. |

---

### 6. Summary Reference Table

| Category | Direct Form | Connector / Pattern | Reported Form |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Yes/No Question** | *"Are you ready?"* | `if / whether` + Subj + Verb | He asked **if I was** ready. |
| **Wh- Question** | *"What time is it?"* | `Wh- word` + Subj + Verb | She asked **what time it was**. |
| **Order / Command**| *"Stop recording!"* | `told/ordered + Obj + to-infinitive` | He ordered us **to stop** recording. |
| **Negative Order** | *"Don't enter!"* | `warned/told + Obj + not to-infinitive` | She warned them **not to enter**. |
| **Polite Request** | *"Please help me."*| `asked + Obj + to-infinitive` | He asked me **to help** him. |

## 💬 Conversational Dialogue

**Context:** Sandra (HR Specialist) is debriefing Nathan (Team Lead) on a candidate interview and subsequent onboarding requests.

* **Sandra:** "Hi Nathan, how did the interview with Lucas go this morning?"
* **Nathan:** "It went very smoothly. First, I asked him **why he wanted to transition** into cloud infrastructure."
* **Sandra:** "What was his answer?"
* **Nathan:** "He explained that he had worked on legacy systems for four years and wanted new technical challenges. Then I asked **if he had any hands-on experience** with container architecture."
* **Sandra:** "Did you discuss start dates and equipment?"
* **Nathan:** "Yes. He asked me **when we would make our final decision**, and I told him **to expect a call** by Thursday."
* **Sandra:** "Did he make any specific requests regarding hardware?"
* **Nathan:** "Yes, he politely asked us **to configure a dual-monitor setup** if he gets the offer."

## ✍️ Practice Exercises

### Exercise A: Report the Questions
Convert the following direct questions into reported speech with the prompt provided.
1. "Where did you purchase this software?" $\rightarrow$ *Julian asked me...*
2. "Do you have any dietary restrictions?" $\rightarrow$ *The flight attendant inquired...*
3. "Why are the servers running slowly today?" $\rightarrow$ *The director asked the IT team...*
4. "Will you be able to attend the conference?" $\rightarrow$ *The coordinator asked whether...*

### Exercise B: Report the Orders and Requests
Transform each imperative or polite request into indirect speech.
5. "Please do not disclose this confidential data," the legal advisor urged the staff.
6. "Sign both copies of the contract and return them by mail," the agent told him.
7. "Could you please review my resume before tomorrow?" Daniel asked Sarah.

---

## 🔑 Self-Check Answer Key

### Exercise A:
1. **Julian asked me where I had purchased that software.** *(Wh- question word order: Subject 'I' + past perfect 'had purchased'; this $\rightarrow$ that).*
2. **The flight attendant inquired if (or whether) I had any dietary restrictions.**
3. **The director asked the IT team why the servers were running slowly that day.** *(Subject 'the servers' precedes verb 'were running'; today $\rightarrow$ that day).*
4. **The coordinator asked whether we would be able to attend the conference.** *(will $\rightarrow$ would).*

### Exercise B:
5. **The legal advisor urged the staff not to disclose that confidential data.** *(Negative request: urged + object + not to + verb).*
6. **The agent told him to sign both copies of the contract and return them by mail.** *(Order: told + object + to + verb).*
7. **Daniel asked Sarah to review his resume before the next day (or the following day).** *(Polite request: asked + object + to + verb).*

## 💡 Practical Tip for Daily Practice
> **The Statement Check:** When speaking or writing indirect questions, pause before the embedded clause and ask: *"Is this word order identical to a normal positive statement?"*
> - Question: *"Where is the exit?"*
> - Statement order: *"...the exit was."*
> - Result: *"He asked where the exit was."* (Never *"where was the exit"*).
