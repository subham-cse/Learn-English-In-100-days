### 2. The Top 10 High-Frequency Foundation Pitfalls

1. **The Double-Do Error in Questions:**
   - ✗ *"Where did you went yesterday?"*
   - ✓ *"Where did you **go** yesterday?"* *(Auxiliary 'did' already carries the past tense; main verb stays base form).*
2. **The Subject Question Auxiliary Blunder:**
   - ✗ *"Who did tell you the news?"*
   - ✓ *"Who **told** you the news?"* *(Subject questions do not use 'did').*
3. **The "Prefer... Than" Mistake:**
   - ✗ *"I prefer coffee than tea."*
   - ✓ *"I prefer coffee **to** tea."*
4. **"Would Rather" with 'To':**
   - ✗ *"I would rather to stay home."*
   - ✓ *"I would rather **stay** home."*
5. **The "Mind" Complement Trap:**
   - ✗ *"Would you mind to close the window?"*
   - ✓ *"Would you mind **closing** the window?"*
6. **Replying to "Thank You" with "Please":**
   - ✗ *"Thank you!" — "Please."*
   - ✓ *"Thank you!" — "**You're very welcome / Happy to help.**"*
7. **The "Aren't I" Exemption:**
   - ✗ *"I am on time, amn't I?"*
   - ✓ *"I am on time, **aren't I**?"*
8. **Movement Preposition Confusion:**
   - ✗ *"We walked across the dark forest."*
   - ✓ *"We walked **through** the dark forest."* *(3D interior volume requires 'through').*
9. **Indirect Question Inversion:**
   - ✗ *"Could you tell me where is the restroom?"*
   - ✓ *"Could you tell me where **the restroom is**?"*
10. **The Negative Tag Polarity Slip:**
    - ✗ *"He doesn't like spicy food, doesn't he?"*
    - ✓ *"He doesn't like spicy food, **does he**?"*

## 💬 Conversational Dialogue: Comprehensive Real-World Scenario

**Context:** Hannah has just arrived in Edinburgh to attend an international academic seminar. At the reception hall, she meets an attendee, Patrick.

> **Patrick:** Good morning! Quite a brisk breeze outside today, isn't it?  
> **Hannah:** It really is! I wasn't quite prepared for Scottish autumn weather. Pardon me, but would you happen to know where the badge collection desk is?  
> **Patrick:** Absolutely. You'll want to head straight down this corridor, cut through the atrium on your right, and it will be directly opposite the auditorium.  
> **Hannah:** Go down the corridor, through the atrium, and opposite the auditorium. Got it! Thank you so much for your help.  
> **Patrick:** Don't mention it! Are you presenting a paper at the conference, or are you attending the panels?  
> **Hannah:** I'm presenting tomorrow morning on computational linguistics. How about you? Who recommended this symposium to you?  
> **Patrick:** My research advisor in Glasgow. By the way, there's a networking lunch at 1:00 PM, but I personally prefer quiet lunches to crowded buffets. Would you rather join a smaller group for local soup down the street?  
> **Hannah:** I'd definitely rather have a quiet lunch than navigate a crowded buffet. That sounds wonderful!  
> **Patrick:** Fantastic. Let's catch up right outside the main exit at 12:50, shall we?  
> **Hannah:** Perfect. See you then!

## ✍️ Practice Exercises: Cumulative Foundation Assessment

### Part A: Error Identification & Correction
Each sentence contains one grammatical or structural error. Identify and rewrite the correct sentence.
1. Could you tell me what time does the train depart?
2. Most people in my office prefer working from home than commuting.
3. Who did write this fascinating research paper?
4. She doesn't speak Italian, doesn't she?
5. I would rather to take the afternoon flight than travel at dawn.

### Part B: Mixed Syntactic Completion
Fill in the blanks with the exact correct word or phrase.
1. Would you mind ________________ (turn) down the volume slightly?
2. To reach the botanical gardens, walk ______________ the suspension bridge and take the first left.
3. Let's wrap up this discussion before lunch, ________________?
4. I am scheduled for the next round of interviews, ________________?
5. I'm afraid modern jazz simply isn't my ________________ of tea.

---

## 🔑 Self-Check Answer Key

### Part A: Error Correction
1. **Could you tell me what time the train departs?** *(Indirect question requires standard S-V word order, not question inversion).*
2. **Most people in my office prefer working from home to commuting.** *(Prefer X to Y).*
3. **Who wrote this fascinating research paper?** *(Subject question: no auxiliary 'did').*
4. **She doesn't speak Italian, does she?** *(Negative main clause requires a positive tag).*
5. **I would rather take the afternoon flight than travel at dawn.** *(Would rather + bare infinitive).*

### Part B: Mixed Syntactic Completion
1. Would you mind **turning** down the volume slightly?
2. Walk **across** the suspension bridge...
3. Let's wrap up this discussion before lunch, **shall we?**
4. I am scheduled for the next round of interviews, **aren't I?**
5. ...isn't my **cup** of tea.
