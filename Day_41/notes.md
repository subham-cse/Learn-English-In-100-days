# Day 41 : Everyday Phrasal Verbs 1

## 📚 Overview
A **phrasal verb** is an idiomatic combination of a standard verb and one or more particles (prepositions or adverbs), producing a meaning distinct from the literal meaning of the individual words. One of the greatest challenges for English learners is understanding **separability**: knowing whether the particle can—or must—be separated from the verb by an object. Today, we demystify the four categories of phrasal verbs, analyze key everyday verbs (*turn on/off, look up, pick up*), and master the indispensable **"Pronoun Placement Rule."**

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Classify phrasal verbs into separable transitive, inseparable transitive, and intransitive.
- Apply the **Pronoun Placement Rule** flawlessly without making word-order errors.
- Use high-frequency daily phrasal verbs (*turn on/off, turn up/down, look up, pick up, put away, throw away*) naturally.
- Recognize how contextual nuances shift the meaning of phrasal verbs (*e.g., pick up a box vs. pick up a language*).

## 📖 Theoretical Breakdown

### 1. Anatomy and Classification of Phrasal Verbs

Phrasal verbs fall into four structural categories:

```
                      ┌─── Transitive (Takes an object) ───┬─── Separable (Can split)
                      │                                    └─── Inseparable (Cannot split)
Phrasal Verbs ────────┤
                      ├─── Intransitive (No object) ─────────── Always Together
                      └─── Three-Word (Verb + Part + Prep) ──── Always Inseparable
```

---

### 2. Separable Phrasal Verbs & The Golden Pronoun Rule

In **separable transitive** phrasal verbs, a direct noun object can appear either **after** the particle or **between** the verb and particle.

#### With Noun Objects:
- Both positions are grammatically correct:
  - *Please **turn on** the air conditioner.* $\checkmark$
  - *Please **turn** the air conditioner **on**.* $\checkmark$
  - *She **looked up** the definition.* $\checkmark$
  - *She **looked** the definition **up**.* $\checkmark$

#### The Golden Pronoun Rule ⚡
When the direct object is an object pronoun (**it, them, him, her, me, us, you**), the pronoun **MUST** go in the middle, between the verb and the particle. Placing a pronoun after the particle is ungrammatical.

| Correct Pronoun Placement | Ungrammatical Placement (Common Mistake) |
| :--- | :--- |
| *Please turn **it** on.* $\checkmark$ | *Please turn on **it**.* ❌ |
| *I looked **it** up in the directory.* $\checkmark$ | *I looked up **it**.* ❌ |
| *She picked **them** up from school.* $\checkmark$ | *She picked up **them**.* ❌ |
| *Throw **it** away; it's broken.* $\checkmark$ | *Throw away **it**.* ❌ |

---

### 3. Inseparable Phrasal Verbs (Transitive)
In **inseparable phrasal verbs**, the object (whether a noun or a pronoun) must **always** follow the particle. The verb and particle cannot be separated under any circumstance:

- *look after* (care for): *She looks after her elderly parents.* / *She looks after them.* *(Never: "looks them after")*
- *run into* (meet unexpectedly): *I ran into Marcus downtown.* / *I ran into him.* *(Never: "ran him into")*
- *get over* (recover from): *He got over the flu.* / *He got over it.* *(Never: "got it over")*

---

### 4. Key Everyday Phrasal Verbs in Focus

| Phrasal Verb | Separable? | Meaning | Practical Example |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **turn on / off** | Yes | Activate / deactivate a device | *Could you turn the projector on? / Turn it off.* |
| **turn up / down** | Yes | Increase / decrease volume or heat | *Please turn down the music; it's distracting.* |
| **look up** | Yes | Search for information in reference | *If you don't know the word, look it up.* |
| **pick up** | Yes | 1. Lift physically<br>2. Collect someone<br>3. Learn casually | 1. *Pick up that pen.*<br>2. *I'll pick you up at 6 PM.*<br>3. *He picked up Spanish during his trip.* |
| **fill out / in** | Yes | Complete a form or document | *Please fill out this registration form.* |
| **throw away** | Yes | Discard into the trash | *Don't throw those receipts away.* |
| **put away** | Yes | Store in its proper location | *Put your tools away when finished.* |
| **give up** | Yes | Abandon a habit or stop trying | *Never give up your ambitions.* |

## 💬 Conversational Dialogue

**Context:** Liam is helping his flatmate Zoe pack up and tidy their shared apartment.

* **Liam:** "Zoe, what should we do with all these old tech magazines on the coffee table?"
* **Zoe:** "Please don't **throw them away**! I still need a few articles for my dissertation."
* **Liam:** "Alright, I'll **put them away** in the bookshelf upstairs. Where should I put this bluetooth speaker?"
* **Zoe:** "You can **turn it off** and pack it into the electronics crate."
* **Liam:** "Got it. By the way, do you know the postal code for our new flat?"
* **Zoe:** "I can't recall off the top of my head. Let me **look it up** on my phone."
* **Liam:** "Thanks. If the delivery van arrives early tomorrow, I'll **pick up** the keys from the leasing office."
* **Zoe:** "Great teamwork! We'll have everything sorted in no time."

## 🔑 Self-Check Answer Key

### Exercise A:
1. **Did you turn them off before leaving?** *(Plural noun "lights" $\rightarrow$ pronoun "them"; must be placed between verb and particle).*
2. **The researcher looked them up online.** *(Plural noun "statistics" $\rightarrow$ "them"; placed in the middle).*
3. **Don't throw them away!**
4. **The courier will pick them up this afternoon.**

### Exercise B:
5. **Incorrect (I)** $\rightarrow$ **Correction:** *"Can you **look it up** in the dictionary?"* *(Pronoun must separate the verb and particle).*
6. **Correct (C)** $\rightarrow$ *Look after* is an inseparable phrasal verb; the pronoun correctly follows the preposition.
7. **Correct (C)** $\rightarrow$ *Turn down* is separable; placing a noun phrase after the particle is natural and fully correct.
8. **Incorrect (I)** $\rightarrow$ **Correction:** *"The coach told them not to **give it up**."* *(Or: "not to give up").*

## 💡 Practical Tip for Daily Practice
> **The Sandwich Rule:** Treat separable phrasal verbs like two slices of bread: the verb is the top slice (*turn, pick, look*), and the particle is the bottom slice (*on, up, off*). When an object is a tiny pronoun (**it, them, him, her**), it is the filling and **MUST be sandwiched in the middle**: *turn [it] on*, never *turn on [it]*.
