# Day 14 : Prepositions of Time & Duration (At/In/On, Since/For/During/By)

## 📚 Overview
Time is the governing framework of human schedules, deadlines, historical periods, and future commitments. English utilizes a structured temporal hierarchy to anchor moments and stretches of time. Understanding the division of temporal prepositions—from punctual points (*at 5 PM*) to calendar units (*on Tuesday*) and vast intervals (*in the 21st century*)—alongside duration markers (*since, for, during, by, until*) will ensure you schedule appointments and discuss timelines with flawless precision.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Apply the chronological pyramid of **AT** (precise clock moments), **ON** (days and specific dates), and **IN** (longer spans, months, years, seasons, centuries).
- Accurately choose between **FOR** (quantity of elapsed time) and **SINCE** (starting point anchor in the past).
- Master the difference between **DURING** (+ noun event) and **FOR** (+ numerical duration).
- Differentiate between **BY** (deadline / no later than) and **UNTIL** (continuous duration up to a stopping threshold).

---

## 📖 Theoretical Breakdown

### 1. The Temporal Pyramid: AT vs. ON vs. IN

```
       /\
      /  \      AT  -> Precise moment / clock time / holiday periods without "Day"
     / AT \            (at 6:30 AM, at noon, at midnight, at sunset, at Christmas)
    /------\
   /   ON   \   ON  -> Calendar days / specific dates / day parts with named day
  /----------\         (on Monday, on October 15th, on my birthday, on Friday morning)
  |    IN    |  IN  -> Broad periods / months / years / seasons / decades / centuries
  |__________|         (in May, in 2026, in the summer, in the 1990s, in 10 minutes [future])
```

#### Special Temporal Nuances:
- *at the weekend* (British English) vs. *on the weekend* (American English)
- *in the morning / in the afternoon / in the evening* BUT *at night*
- Future timeframe meaning "after this duration": *"I will call you **in five minutes**."*

---

### 2. Duration & Timeline Anchors: FOR vs. SINCE vs. DURING

| Preposition | Meaning / Formula | Example Sentence |
|---|---|---|
| **FOR** | Measures the **total quantity / span** of elapsed time (*for + number + time unit*) | We have lived here **for eight years**. |
| **SINCE** | Marks the **specific starting anchor** in the past until now (*since + starting point*) | We have lived here **since 2018**. |
| **DURING** | Occurring throughout or within a **named event/period** (*during + noun phrase*) | She took notes **during the lecture**. |

> [!WARNING]
> Never say: *"I slept during eight hours"* ❌ &rarr; say: *"I slept **for eight hours**"* ✅ (numerical duration requires *for*).  
> Never say: *"I lived here since eight years"* ❌ &rarr; say: *"I lived here **for eight years**"* ✅.

---

### 3. Deadlines vs. Continuous Persistence: BY vs. UNTIL

This pair causes frequent confusion in professional communication:

```
BY (+ point in time)      -> DEADLINE / Completion point. The action happens before or at that moment.
                             "Please submit the report BY 5:00 PM." (4:30 is fine; 5:01 is late)

UNTIL (+ point in time)   -> CONTINUOUS DURATION. An ongoing action persists up to that moment and stops.
                             "I will be working in the office UNTIL 5:00 PM." (Continuous work stops at 5)
```

**Negative Context with UNTIL:**
- *"We cannot release the software **until** the security audit is finished."* (No action before that threshold).

## 💬 Conversational Dialogue

**Context:** Rachel and Aaron are synchronizing project schedules and client milestone delivery dates.

**Aaron:** Rachel, when do we need to deliver the finalized design prototypes to the client?  
**Rachel:** The client contract stipulates that the files must be uploaded by Friday at 3:00 PM.  
**Aaron:** Understood. How long has our engineering group been testing the beta release?  
**Rachel:** They have been running stress tests since last Wednesday. In fact, they worked straight through the weekend.  
**Aaron:** Will they continue testing tomorrow?  
**Rachel:** Yes, the automated testing script will run until midnight tonight. Then in the morning, we will analyze the performance logs.  
**Aaron:** Sounds like a solid timeline. I noticed that during the initial trial run, memory consumption stayed very stable.  
**Rachel:** That is encouraging. We will wrap up our review in two hours and submit everything well ahead of the deadline!

### Exercise 1: Choose AT, ON, or IN

2. The regional office was founded ________ 2014.
3. We have an executive committee meeting scheduled ________ Wednesday afternoon.
4. I prefer reading quietly ________ night before sleeping.

1. The museum will remain closed to visitors ________ next Monday for renovations.
2. She has been studying neurobiology ________ she graduated from high school.
3. Please make sure the quarterly invoices are processed ________ the end of the day.
4. The lights flickered multiple times ________ the thunderstorm.
5. They traveled through South America ________ three months.

## 🔑 Self-Check Answer Key
**Exercise 1:**
1. *at* (Exact clock time)
2. *in* (Year)
3. *on* (Specific day + time of day)
4. *at* (Standard fixed collocation: *at night*)

**Exercise 2:**
1. *until* (Continuous state of remaining closed until that date)
2. *since* (Starting point in the past)
3. *by* (Deadline: no later than end of day)
4. *during* (Within a named event/noun)
5. *for* (Numerical duration / span of 3 months)

---

## 💡 Practical Tip for Daily Practice
The **"By" vs. "Until" Test**: If an event happens **once** as a deadline, use **by** (*"Send the email by 4 PM"*). If the action continues **unbroken** like a movie or shift, use **until** (*"Wait here until 4 PM"*).
