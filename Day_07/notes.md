# Day 07 : Essential Regular Verbs & Conjugation Patterns

## 📚 Overview
Verbs are the engine of every English sentence. Regular verbs represent the bedrock of English communication because they follow predictable patterns when shifting across tenses. Understanding how regular verbs conjugate in the present, past, and continuous forms—along with mastering the subtle pronunciation rules of the `-ed` ending—will instantly boost your grammatical accuracy and spoken fluency.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Identify regular verbs and conjugate them accurately across Simple Present, Simple Past, and Present Continuous.
- Apply spelling rules when appending `-s/-es`, `-ed`, and `-ing` suffixes.
- Master the 3 distinct phonological pronunciations of the past tense suffix `-ed`: `/t/`, `/d/`, and `/ɪd/`.
- Construct positive sentences, negative statements, and questions using auxiliary verbs (`do/does`, `did`).

---

## 📖 Theoretical Breakdown

### 1. Base Forms vs. Inflections
A regular verb forms its past tense and past participle simply by adding `-ed` or `-d` to the base form (infinitive without *to*).

| Verb Form | Name | Example (Work) | Example (Study) | Example (Plan) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **V1** | Base / Infinitive | work | study | plan |
| **V1 + s/es** | 3rd Person Singular Present | works | studies | plans |
| **V-ing** | Present Participle / Continuous | working | studying | planning |
| **V2** | Simple Past | worked | studied | planned |
| **V3** | Past Participle | worked | studied | planned |

---

### 2. Spelling Rules for Suffixes

#### A. Adding `-ed` for Past Tense
1. **Standard verbs:** Add `-ed` (e.g., *walk &rarr; walked*, *call &rarr; called*).
2. **Verbs ending in `-e`:** Just add `-d` (e.g., *live &rarr; lived*, *decide &rarr; decided*).
3. **Consonant + `y`:** Change `y` to `i` and add `-ed` (e.g., *study &rarr; studied*, *carry &rarr; carried*).
   *Exception: Vowel + `y` just adds `-ed` (play &rarr; played).*
4. **1-Syllable verbs with 1 Consonant-Vowel-Consonant (CVC):** Double the final consonant before `-ed` (e.g., *stop &rarr; stopped*, *rob &rarr; robbed*).

#### B. Adding `-s` / `-es` in Present Simple (He / She / It)
1. **General:** Add `-s` (*works, runs, talks*).
2. **Ending in `-s`, `-sh`, `-ch`, `-x`, `-z`, `-o`:** Add `-es` (*watches, washes, fixes, goes*).
3. **Consonant + `y`:** Change `y` to `i` and add `-es` (*cries, carries*).

---

### 3. The 3 Pronunciations of `-ed` (Crucial for Spoken English)
The `-ed` ending is never pronounced randomly. It strictly depends on the final sound of the base verb:

| Pronunciation | Rule / Trigger Sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **/ɪd/** or **/əd/** | When base verb ends in **T** or **D** sounds (creates an extra syllable) | *waited, decided, needed, invited, started* |
| **/t/** | When base verb ends in **voiceless consonants** (`/p/`, `/k/`, `/f/`, `/s/`, `/ʃ/`, `/tʃ/`) | *worked, laughed, washed, watched, helped, kissed* |
| **/d/** | When base verb ends in **voiced sounds** (all vowels + `/b/`, `/g/`, `/v/`, `/z/`, `/m/`, `/n/`, `/l/`, `/r/`) | *played, cleaned, loved, offered, opened, grabbed* |

---

### 4. Sentence Structures with Auxiliaries
When forming negatives and questions in the past or present, the main regular verb **returns to its base form (V1)** because the auxiliary (`do / does / did`) carries the tense marker.

- **Affirmative Past:** *She completed the report yesterday.*
- **Negative Past:** *She did not (didn't) complete the report yesterday.* (NOT *didn't completed*)
- **Interrogative Past:** *Did she complete the report yesterday?*

## 💬 Conversational Dialogue

**Context:** Maya and Liam are reviewing yesterday's team project progress at their workplace.

**Maya:** Good morning Liam! Did you manage to finalize the client presentation yesterday?  
**Liam:** Yes, I worked on it late into the afternoon. I gathered all the metrics and organized the slides.  
**Maya:** Excellent! Did Sarah review the marketing budget figures before you submitted them?  
**Liam:** She did. She suggested a few revisions, so I adjusted the estimates accordingly.  
**Maya:** Perfect. I noticed the client accepted our proposal this morning. They loved the clean design!  
**Liam:** That is wonderful news. It was hard work, but the extra effort paid off.

## 💡 Practical Tip for Daily Practice
Touch your vocal cords with two fingers when saying base verbs. If your throat vibrates on the last sound (voiced, like *call, rub, buzz*), the `-ed` sounds like `/d/`. If there is no throat vibration (voiceless, like *stop, walk, brush*), the `-ed` sounds like `/t/`. If the verb already ends in `/t/` or `/d/`, give it an extra beat: `/ɪd/`!
